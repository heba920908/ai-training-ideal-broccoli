# Style Guide Reference

## Brand Voice at a Glance

| Attribute | Do | Don't |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Voice | Explain plainly and own the action | Hide behind corporate ceremony |
| Tone | Be calm, useful, and empathetic | Hype, blame, or patronize |
| Sentences | One idea, usually under 20 words | Stack clauses |
| Vocabulary | Use familiar, specific words | Use jargon to sound impressive |
| Point of view | You for the reader, we for our work | Use impersonal passive phrasing |
| Contractions | Use natural forms such as we're | Force a stiff legal tone |

## Content Types

### Emails

Subject lines: aim for 50 characters or fewer; state the outcome or action.
Avoid ALL CAPS, vague "Important update", clickbait, and misleading urgency.
Body: open with the outcome, give necessary context, then one clear call to
action on its own line. Label links with the action, not "click here".
Sign off with "Thanks," and a team or person name when appropriate.
Transactional messages stay factual. Marketing explains a concrete benefit without
unproved claims. Support acknowledges the difficulty, then gives the solution.

### Blog Posts

Use one H1, H2 sections, and H3 subsections without skipped levels.
Keep the introduction under 80 words and state what the reader will gain.
Aim for roughly grade-eight reading level, explain necessary technical terms,
and expand acronyms on first use. Headings use sentence case and describe an
outcome, not a generic label such as "Overview". Use examples, short paragraphs,
and descriptive links. Cite claims that need evidence.

### Social Media

LinkedIn: brand limit 1,200 characters (below the platform maximum), including
spaces and hashtags. Open with a useful insight in the first two lines, not
"We're thrilled to announce". Use short paragraphs, one practical takeaway,
and one relevant question or action. Use zero to three specific hashtags at the
end; no hashtag clouds. No decorative emoji; an essential symbol also needs
a text equivalent. Do not invent customer results or performance statistics.

Twitter/X: stay within 280 characters, including links as rendered by the platform.
Open with the point, include one idea and at most one action. Use zero to two
relevant hashtags and no decorative emoji. Do not compress prose into unreadable
abbreviations. Check the character count before returning a final post.

### Support / Help Content

Be empathy-first and solution-first: acknowledge the impact in one sentence,
then show the next useful action. Use partnering phrases such as "Let's check"
or "We can help", not "You failed to" or "Obviously".
Use numbered steps, one action per step, and bold exact UI labels such as
**Settings**. State expected results and a fallback when a step does not work.
Close with a specific offer to help without promising an unverified resolution
time. Never request passwords or secrets in a reply.

## Grammar & Mechanics

- Punctuation: use the Oxford comma. Prefer a period, comma, or colon to an em dash.
	Avoid exclamation marks in support and transactions; at most one in marketing.
- Capitalization: sentence case for headings and subjects. Preserve official product names.
- Numbers: spell out one through nine in prose, but use digits for quantities,
	dates, measurements, percentages, and numbered steps. Use separators for thousands.
- Inclusive language: singular "they" when pronouns are unknown; neutral job titles;
	"allowlist" and "blocklist"; "primary" and "replica". Do not assume gender,
	age, nationality, disability, or access to a device.
- Preserve facts, dates, names, constraints, and uncertainty. Plain language must
	not turn an estimate into a commitment.
